## L5: the plan hash survives both Pythons

**Rule.** `kit_corner(v)` is `0.0` when `|v| < KIT_SNAP` (1e-6), otherwise `v`. `abs(-0.0)` is 0, so −0.0 becomes 0.0
too. Every corner goes through it before `struct.pack`.

**The guard.** It now asks whether the packed float32 of the snapped `v ± kit_slack(v)` differs, and refuses the corner if
it does. That catches a float32 rounding edge and also `KIT_SNAP`'s own edge.
- `kit_slack` = 16 ulps of max(|v|, 1). That is measured at the piece's scale, not relative to the value, because a value
  made by cancellation carries its inputs' absolute error. This is the hole the reviewer found.
- Measured across the two Pythons, every one of the 45,567 kit corners compared in exact hex:
  - 666 corners differ, by at most **2 ulps at that scale** (1.78e-15 studs);
  - none is −0.0 on either Python;
  - none is 0 on one Python and non-zero on the other today.
- So the slack has an 8× margin, and **no shipped corner is refused**.
- The old guard (16 ulps of v itself) would not catch a corner 5e-16 past a float32 edge near 0.1.

**The test** (`forestcheck.hash_rules`, run by every `forest.py check`):
- a corner of cos(π/2) × 0.1 (6.12e-18) packs like 0.0;
- a −0.0 corner packs like 0.0;
- a corner 5e-16 past a float32 rounding edge near 0.1 is refused;
- a corner on `KIT_SNAP`'s edge is refused.

**Mutants** (`forest.py mutants`, each CAUGHT by its own line):

- "slack at the corner's scale" (the fix round's guard);
